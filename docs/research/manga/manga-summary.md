## Manga Creation Pipeline Summary (Synthesized from Sources)

### Stage 1: Concept & Script (名前 / Nāme)
- Write the story script, dividing it into arcs and episodes
- Plan main plot points and pacing
- Script dialogue and scene descriptions
- Use tools: Google Docs, paper, dedicated manga storyboard apps (MediBang's MangaName)

### Stage 2: Storyboard / Thumbnails (ネーム / Nēmu or サムネイル)
- In manga production, this stage is called "nēmu" (ネーム)
- Rough page layouts showing panel arrangement, character positions, flow
- No detail required - just composition blocking
- The nēmu stage is critical; Japanese editors review it before full production begins
- Digital: rough sketches on a separate layer in Clip Studio Paint or MediBang Paint
- Reading direction: Japanese manga reads right-to-left; Western comics left-to-right; Webtoons scroll vertically

### Stage 3: Rough Sketch / Base (下書き / Shitajiki or ベース)
- Detailed rough pencil sketches of all panels
- Character poses, expressions, background elements
- Traditional: pencil on manga paper (usually B4 size: 257mm × 364mm with specific margin lines)
- Digital: on a dedicated sketch layer with reduced opacity (often blue or grey tinted)
- In digital workflow: Layer Property > Effect > Layer color to change sketch layer color for visibility during inking

### Stage 4: Pencils (鉛筆 / Enpitsu)
- In traditional manga: B4 manuscript paper is the standard
- Detailed pencil drawings refining the rough sketch
- In digital workflow: often the sketch/rough and pencil stages are combined
- Character sheets provide consistent reference during this stage

### Stage 5: Inks / Line Art (ペン入れ / Pen-ire)
- The most critical technical stage in manga production
- Traditional tools:
  - G-pen (Gペン): Variable line weight, excellent for outlines and character lines
  - Mapping pen (マルペン / Maru nib): Fine, detailed lines (hair, eyes, small details)
  - Turnip pen (カブラペン): Softer lines than G-pen
  - Brush: Widest line variation, hardest to control
  - Multiliner/technical pens (0.1, 0.3, 0.5mm): Consistent line width, good for backgrounds
  - India ink (墨汁): Standard black ink used with dip pens
- Digital tools (Clip Studio Paint):
  - G-pen tool (digital): Replicates variable-width G-pen behavior
  - Mapping pen tool: Thin precise lines
  - India Ink Smooth brush: Textured brush with line variance
  - Vector layers preferred for line art (easily editable, resolution-independent)
  - Raster layers for coloring
- Inking techniques:
  - Spotted blacks: Large areas of solid black to show values and depth
  - Feathering: Parallel lines thinning from thick to thin to create tonal gradation
  - Line weight variation: Thick lines on shadow side, thin on light side
  - Effect lines (speed lines, focus lines, impact lines)

### Stage 6: Tones / Screentones (スクリーントーン / Screentone)
- Traditional manga is predominantly black and white (monochrome)
- Screentones add gray values and textures without shading in full gray
- Traditional screentones: Adhesive dot-pattern sheets applied by hand
  - Cut to shape with a craft knife
  - Pressed down to adhere
  - Excess trimmed
  - Time-consuming process
  - Must be repurchased as they are used up
- Digital screentones (Clip Studio Paint):
  - Built into the software
  - Applied to a selection via paste
  - Customizable dot size, angle, density
  - Tone layers can be converted to dot patterns for print
  - Can also simulate "moiré" effect of traditional screentones
  - Converting 3D models to line art with tones: right-click > Convert to Lines and Tones
- Types of screentone patterns: Dot (most common), Cross-hatch, Gradient, Decorative patterns (flowers, stars, etc.)

### Stage 7: Lettering (文字入れ / Moji-ire)
- Adding dialogue, sound effects (SFX/onomatopoeia), and narration
- Speech bubbles (フキダシ / Fukidashi):
  - Round/oval: Normal conversation
  - Jagged/spiky: Shouting, anger, surprise
  - Cloud-shaped: Thought bubbles
  - Wavy: Fear, uncertainty, whispering
  - Sea urchin flash (集中線フキダシ): Shock, revelation
- Traditional: Lettering with a mapping pen or brush pen; speech bubble shapes drawn by hand
- Digital (Clip Studio Paint):
  - Balloon Sub Tool for speech bubbles
  - Flash Sub Tool for impact/thought bubbles
  - Text Sub Tool for dialogue entry
  - Font selection: Clean fonts for normal speech; bold/italic for emphasis
  - Adjustable bubble shape, thickness, brush shape via Operation Tool
- Sound effects (擬音語 / Giongo, 擬態語 / Gitaigo): Japanese manga uses extensive onomatopoeia integrated into artwork
- Narration boxes: Rectangular caption boxes for narrative text

### Stage 8: Finishing Details (仕上げ / Shiage)
- Final corrections and polish
- White-out corrections (traditional) or digital erasing
- Highlight addition (white ink over black areas for traditional; Add/Glow/Screen layers in digital)
- Effect lines and action lines
- Background completion
- Digital: Auto-actions for polish effects (e.g., LJ Smooth Outline for glow effect around characters)
- Border effects on sound effects

### Stage 9: Export / Print Preparation
- Traditional manga submitted to editor as physical pages (B4 paper)
- Digital export settings:
  - Resolution: 600 DPI minimum for print; 300 DPI acceptable for digital-only
  - File format: PSD, TIFF for print; PNG, JPEG for web
  - For magazines/tankōbon: Grayscale mode, 600 DPI
  - For webtoons: RGB mode, 72-150 DPI, 800px wide (standard webtoon width)
  - Webtoon vertical divisions: 800 x 1280 pixel segments
  - Clip Studio Paint: File > Special Export > Export Webtoon (EX); or File > Export (Single Layer) (Pro)
  - Screentone layers must be properly set to avoid moiré in print

## Key Manga Creation Tools

### Traditional (Physical)
- B4 manga manuscript paper with pre-printed margin and bleed guides
- India ink (Pilot, Kuretake, Deleter brands)
- Dip pens: G-pen, Maru (mapping) pen, Kabura (turnip) pen
- Screentone sheets (Deleter, IC)
- Light box for tracing
- T-square/ruler and circle template
- Correction fluid (white-out)
- N-11 or similar manga pencils

### Digital Software
- **Clip Studio Paint (CSP)** - Industry standard; formerly Manga Studio; made by Celsys
  - EX version: Full multi-page management, story function
  - Pro version: Single page work
  - Has dedicated manga tools: panel/frame tools, balloon tools, screentone library, G-pen
- **MediBang Paint** - Free cross-platform (Windows, Mac, iPad, iPhone, Android)
  - Cloud storage for collaboration
  - Built-in manga resources and brushes
  - MangaName app for storyboarding
  - JUMP PAINT: Special version co-developed with Shueisha for Weekly Jump manga
- **Adobe Photoshop** - Can be used but lacks dedicated manga tools; requires workarounds for panels and screentones

### Digital Hardware
- Drawing tablet: Wacom Intuos/Cintiq, Huion, XP-Pen
- iPad + Apple Pencil (with Clip Studio Paint or MediBang Paletta)
- Android tablet

## Publication Pipeline (Print Manga)

1. **Serialization in magazine**: Weekly or monthly anthology magazine publication
   - Major magazines: Weekly Shōnen Jump, Weekly Shōnen Magazine, Weekly Shōnen Sunday, etc.
   - Magazines printed on cheap newsprint
   - 20-40 pages per chapter, typically in black and white (some color opening pages)

2. **Tankōbon compilation**: After sufficient chapters accumulate, compiled into paperback volumes
   - Standard size: B6 (12.8 × 18.2 cm) or A5 (14.8 × 21.0 cm)
   - Higher quality paper than magazine printing
   - Usually 8-12 chapters per volume (approximately 200 pages)
   - Published under imprints: Jump Comics (Shueisha), Shōnen Magazine Comics (Kodansha), etc.

3. **Special editions**: Aizōban, kanzenban, bunkoban, wide-ban reissues
