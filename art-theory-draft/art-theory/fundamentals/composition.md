# Composition

**Status:** Work in progress.

Composition is the arrangement of visual elements so that the image communicates hierarchy, guides the eye, and supports the intended statement. It is solved most effectively at the thumbnail stage and remains the primary reference for every later decision.

## Core Components

### Elements
- Line, shape, value mass, colour mass, texture, space (positive/negative), scale, and direction.

### Principles (relationships among elements)
- **Hierarchy / Emphasis** — one dominant area that receives the strongest contrast or isolation.
- **Balance** — distribution of visual weight (symmetrical, asymmetrical, or radial).
- **Rhythm & Repetition** — repeated intervals that create movement or stability.
- **Unity & Variety** — coherence without monotony.
- **Proportion & Scale** — relative size relationships that reinforce hierarchy.
- **Contrast** — differences in value, size, direction, texture, or colour that create focus.

### Gestalt Grouping
Proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, and figure–ground relationships determine how the eye first organises the image.

## Practical Decision Criteria

1. **Value notan first.** Reduce the image to two or three value families. If the notan is weak, later colour and detail will not rescue the design.
2. **Silhouette readability.** The major shapes should remain legible when the image is reduced to pure black and white.
3. **Entry and path.** Identify where the eye enters and the path it is intended to travel. Avoid accidental equal attractions that compete with the focal area.
4. **Negative space.** Treat empty areas as active shapes that support or contradict the positive forms.
5. **Cropping and edge.** The four edges of the frame are part of the composition; intentional cropping can strengthen or destroy hierarchy.

## Common Failure Modes

- Multiple competing centres of equal contrast.
- Tangents (shapes that touch edges or each other in ways that flatten space).
- Uniform distribution of detail that eliminates hierarchy.
- Solving colour or rendering before the value structure is locked.

## Relationship to Pipeline

Composition is the primary concern of stages 01–03 and remains the reference for all later stages. Changes to major shape relationships after the value study are expensive and should be treated as a conscious return to an earlier stage.
