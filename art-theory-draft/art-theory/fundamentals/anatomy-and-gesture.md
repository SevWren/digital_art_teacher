# Anatomy and Gesture

**Status:** Work in progress.

For figurative work, anatomy supplies the internal structure that makes poses believable; gesture supplies the overall action, weight, and rhythm that make the figure feel alive. Both are required; anatomy without gesture is stiff, gesture without anatomy is vague.


## Plain-English Guide

### What this is

When drawing a person or creature, the first and most important mark is the overall lean and movement of the whole body — the big flowing action before any individual part. The second concern is knowing where the body's fixed bony landmarks sit so that poses look real and convincing.

### Why it matters for your painting

If the overall body lean is wrong from the start, adding careful detail to the arms and legs will not fix it — the figure will look stiff or like separate parts assembled together. The whole-body action has to be right before anything smaller matters.

### The one thing to remember

Draw the whole-body lean as one flowing stroke before touching any individual part. Arms, legs, and face all come after that stroke is placed.

### Common beginner pitfall

Drawing the head or a hand in careful detail first, then trying to fit a body around it — the figure ends up looking like separate pieces glued together rather than one person in motion.

## Gesture

Gesture is the first, most economical statement of the pose:

- Overall action line or “line of action”
- Weight distribution and balance
- Major directional thrusts of torso, limbs, and head
- Rhythm and flow between forms

Gesture is solved early (stage 04) and remains the reference for all subsequent anatomical refinement.

## Structural Anatomy (Artistic)

Focus on landmarks and mass relationships rather than medical nomenclature:

- Skeletal landmarks that show on the surface (clavicle, ASIS, greater trochanter, knees, elbows, etc.)
- Major muscle groups as form masses that change shape with action
- Joint types and their natural range of motion
- Proportions of the average adult figure as a starting reference, adjusted for the specific character and pose

## Decision Criteria

1. Capture the gesture and weight first; lock the overall action before refining individual limbs.
2. Construct the torso and pelvis as related volumes before attaching limbs.
3. Check foreshortening against the underlying construction rather than against a memorised flat view.
4. Verify that the pose is in balance (or intentionally off-balance) by locating the centre of gravity relative to the support.

## Common Failure Modes

- Stiff, “assembled” figures that lack a unifying action line.
- Limbs attached without regard to the orientation of the torso and pelvis.
- Ignoring weight and balance, so the figure appears to float or tip.
- Over-detailing muscles before the major masses and gesture are correct.
